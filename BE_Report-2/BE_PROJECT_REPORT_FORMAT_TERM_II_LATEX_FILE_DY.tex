% ===========================  ABSTRACT  ===================================
{\bfseries \fontsize{14}{12}\selectfont \centerline{Abstract}
\vspace*{2\baselineskip}}
\setlength{\parindent}{11mm}

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder whose early
detection remains challenging because cardinal motor symptoms appear only after
substantial neuronal loss. Speech and voice impairment (hypokinetic dysarthria)
is among the earliest non-motor markers, making acoustic analysis an attractive,
non-invasive screening modality. This project presents the design and
implementation of a web-based Parkinson's disease detection platform that
combines speech analysis with a K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) classifier.

The system is built as a full-stack application: a React (Vite + Tailwind CSS)
frontend, a Flask REST API backend with JWT authentication and role-based access
control, and an SQLite database. Voice samples are processed to extract the 22
acoustic biomarkers of the UCI Parkinson's dataset --- fundamental-frequency
measures, jitter, shimmer, noise ratios (NHR, HNR) and nonlinear dynamical
measures (RPDE, DFA, spread1, spread2, D2, PPE). A KNN classifier
(k = 5, Euclidean distance, distance weighting) trained on the UCI dataset (195
recordings, 31 subjects) achieves approximately 92\% accuracy, 90\% precision,
93--100\% recall and an F1-score above 91\% with an average inference latency of
about 180 ms.

Beyond a standalone predictor, the finalized system is a small healthcare
platform with three roles --- patient, doctor and administrator. Patients
register, record or upload a sustained vowel, view their screening reports,
locate nearby hospitals through an OpenStreetMap-based finder, and book
appointments with verified neurologists. Doctors review patient reports, add
diagnosis notes and manage appointment requests after admin approval. The
contribution of this work is the integration of an optimized KNN speech
classifier into an accessible, deployable, role-aware web platform, addressing
the gap between research prototypes and practical screening tools.

\chapter{Introduction and Literature Survey}

\section{Introduction}
Parkinson's disease (PD) is a chronic, progressive neurodegenerative disorder
that primarily affects the dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra region
of the brain. The loss of dopamine-producing neurons disrupts coordinated
movement and motor control. According to the World Health Organization,
Parkinson's disease affects approximately 10 million individuals worldwide, with
incidence rising significantly with age, making early detection a critical public
health priority.

Clinical manifestations are categorized into motor symptoms (resting tremor,
bradykinesia, rigidity, postural instability --- collectively the TRAP symptoms)
and non-motor symptoms (cognitive impairment, depression, sleep disturbances,
sensory abnormalities). Motor symptoms typically appear only after 60--80\% of
dopaminergic neurons have degenerated, indicating that substantial irreversible
damage has already occurred at clinical presentation.

Speech and voice disorders, collectively termed hypokinetic dysarthria, are among
the earliest and most prevalent non-motor symptoms; vocal impairments can manifest
up to five years before overt motor symptoms. Acoustic characteristics of
Parkinsonian speech can be quantified objectively: fundamental-frequency
perturbations (jitter) reflect instability of vocal-fold vibration; amplitude
perturbations (shimmer) indicate variability of vocal intensity; the
Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR) quantifies additive noise; and nonlinear
dynamical measures (RPDE, DFA, PPE) capture the complex dynamics of vocal-fold
oscillation, which are significantly altered in PD.

The integration of machine learning with speech signal processing enables
objective, non-invasive and scalable screening. The K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)
algorithm is non-parametric, inherently interpretable through neighbourhood
analysis, and requires minimal training time, making it well suited for
web-based deployment. This project implements a web-based PD detection platform
comprising a React frontend for voice collection, a Flask backend for
preprocessing and feature extraction, and a KNN classifier trained on the UCI
Parkinson's dataset.

\section{Acoustic Biomarkers in Parkinson's Disease}
The diagnostic value of speech analysis rests on a set of well-studied acoustic
biomarkers, each capturing a distinct physiological consequence of dopaminergic
depletion on the respiratory--phonatory--articulatory system. These 22 measures
form the feature vector classified by the system.

\textbf{Fundamental frequency (F0) measures.} The fundamental frequency is the rate
of vocal-fold vibration. The mean, maximum and minimum F0 (MDVP:Fo, Fhi, Flo)
characterize pitch and its range. Parkinsonian speech typically exhibits
\emph{monopitch} --- a reduced range of F0 --- reflecting impaired laryngeal motor
control.

\textbf{Jitter measures.} Jitter quantifies cycle-to-cycle variation in fundamental
frequency, i.e.\ short-term frequency instability of the vocal folds. The dataset
includes relative jitter (MDVP:Jitter \%), absolute jitter (MDVP:Jitter(Abs)),
the relative average perturbation (MDVP:RAP), the five-point period perturbation
quotient (MDVP:PPQ) and the average absolute difference of consecutive periods
(Jitter:DDP). Elevated jitter indicates unstable phonation, a hallmark of
Parkinsonian dysphonia.

\textbf{Shimmer measures.} Shimmer quantifies cycle-to-cycle variation in
amplitude, i.e.\ instability of vocal intensity. It is measured as local shimmer
(MDVP:Shimmer), shimmer in decibels (MDVP:Shimmer(dB)), the three-, five- and
eleven-point amplitude perturbation quotients (Shimmer:APQ3, APQ5, MDVP:APQ) and
the average absolute difference of consecutive amplitudes (Shimmer:DDA). Increased
shimmer reflects breathy, unsteady voice quality.

\textbf{Noise ratios.} The Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR) and the Noise-to-Harmonics
Ratio (NHR) express the balance between periodic (harmonic) energy and aperiodic
(noise) energy in the voice signal. Incomplete vocal-fold closure in Parkinson's
disease increases turbulent noise, lowering HNR and raising NHR.

\textbf{Nonlinear dynamical measures.} Because vocal-fold oscillation is a nonlinear
dynamical process, linear measures alone are insufficient. Recurrence Period
Density Entropy (RPDE) quantifies the periodicity/uncertainty of the signal;
Detrended Fluctuation Analysis (DFA) captures long-range correlations and fractal
scaling of the breath-noise; the Pitch Period Entropy (PPE) measures the entropy of
the perceptually-scaled pitch deviation after removing healthy variation; the
correlation dimension (D2) estimates the complexity of the underlying attractor;
and spread1/spread2 capture nonlinear measures of fundamental-frequency variation.
These features are among the most discriminative for Parkinson's detection, as
confirmed by the feature-importance analysis in Chapter~4.

\section{Problem Statement}
Early diagnosis of Parkinson's disease is constrained by several limitations:
subjectivity and expertise dependency of clinical assessment (UPDRS, Hoehn and
Yahr scales); delayed detection because motor symptoms appear late; high cost and
poor accessibility of specialist consultations and neuroimaging (e.g.\ DaTscan);
the lack of validated, objective, non-invasive biomarkers; the absence of
scalable population-level screening tools; and the gap between research
prototypes and deployable systems. This project addresses these challenges by
developing an accessible, web-based detection system that uses speech analysis
and KNN classification, eliminating the need for specialised expertise and
enabling remote, objective screening on standard hardware.

\section{Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item To develop a web application for voice data collection using React for the
frontend and Flask for the backend, enabling remote recording and submission of
speech samples.
\item To implement audio preprocessing and feature extraction that convert raw
speech into clinically relevant acoustic biomarkers (fundamental frequency,
jitter, shimmer, HNR/NHR and nonlinear measures RPDE, DFA, PPE).
\item To develop and optimize a KNN classifier trained on the UCI Parkinson's
dataset to distinguish healthy from Parkinsonian speech with high accuracy and
low inference latency.
\item To perform systematic hyperparameter optimization (k-value, distance
metric, weighting strategy) to maximize classification performance.
\item To implement a complete web deployment architecture integrating the
frontend, backend and machine-learning model.
\item To evaluate performance using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and
AUC-ROC and compare against the literature.
\item To design an intuitive interface presenting results clearly to
non-technical users.
\item To extend the predictor into a role-based healthcare platform with patient,
doctor and administrator workflows, hospital discovery and appointment booking.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope of the Project}
\textbf{Inclusions:} a full-stack web application (React frontend, Flask REST API
backend, Python ML pipeline); audio preprocessing and 22-feature extraction; KNN
classification with hyperparameter optimization; training and validation on the
UCI Parkinson's dataset (195 recordings, 31 subjects --- 23 PD, 8 healthy);
role-based access control (patient/doctor/admin); SQLite persistence with
screening history; an OpenStreetMap-based hospital finder; and appointment
booking between patients and verified doctors.

\textbf{Exclusions:} prospective clinical validation against patient cohorts;
multi-language speech analysis and connected/conversational speech (the system
focuses on sustained vowel /a/ phonation, the most validated PD speech task);
EHR/telemedicine integration; and native mobile/embedded deployment. The system
is a screening aid, not a diagnostic instrument, and does not replace
neurological consultation.

\textbf{Research Gap.} The survey reveals: (1) limited web-based deployment ---
most solutions are command-line prototypes; (2) suboptimal KNN implementations
that use default parameters without systematic tuning; (3) incomplete feature
integration (linear \emph{or} nonlinear features, rarely both); (4) absence of
full-stack integration of recording, processing, inference and visualization;
and (5) poor accessibility and usability for non-technical stakeholders. This
project addresses these gaps with a tuned KNN, a unified 22-feature vector, and a
complete, accessible, role-aware web platform.

\subsection{Detailed Review of Key Contributions}
\textbf{Little et al.\ (2007, 2009).} The foundational work of Little and
colleagues introduced the dysphonia measures that underpin this entire field.
Their 2007 study demonstrated that nonlinear recurrence and fractal scaling
properties (RPDE and DFA) discriminate disordered from healthy voices, and their
2009 work established the suitability of these dysphonia measurements for the
telemonitoring of Parkinson's disease. The 22-feature UCI dataset used in this
project is a direct product of that research. Their reported accuracy of roughly
91\% with support vector machines set the benchmark that subsequent studies,
including this one, are compared against.

\textbf{Tsanas et al.\ (2010).} Building on Little's measures, Tsanas et al.\
applied feature-selection algorithms together with support vector regression and
classification to predict UPDRS scores non-invasively from sustained phonation.
Their work confirmed that a compact subset of acoustic features carries most of
the discriminative information, motivating the systematic feature treatment and
standardization adopted here.

\textbf{Benba et al.\ (2016) and Dubey et al.\ (2020).} These studies are the most
directly comparable to the present work because both employ K-Nearest Neighbors.
Benba et al.\ reported 87.5\% accuracy with a default $k=5$ Euclidean
configuration, while Dubey et al.\ achieved 91.2\% using a Minkowski metric with
$p=3$. Neither performed exhaustive hyperparameter optimization or combined linear
and nonlinear features in a single standardized vector, which this project
identifies as the principal reason its tuned KNN exceeds their results.

\textbf{Sakar et al.\ (2019).} This comparative analysis evaluated multiple speech
signal-processing algorithms and an ensemble of SVM, KNN and Random Forest,
reaching 93.8\% accuracy on an extended dataset using the tunable Q-factor wavelet
transform. While accurate, the ensemble is heavier and less interpretable than a
single tuned KNN, and the study remained a research prototype without deployment.

\textbf{Orozco-Arroyave et al.\ (2014, 2021).} Their deep-learning approaches
(including CNN and LSTM architectures) achieved the highest reported accuracy
(around 94\%), but at the cost of substantial computational requirements, large
training-data demands and reduced interpretability. For a web-deployable,
low-resource screening tool, these trade-offs favour the lighter instance-based
classifier used here.

\textbf{Prasad et al.\ (2020) and Solana-Lavalle et al.\ (2020).} These works
explored Random Forest with MFCC features and combinations of linear and nonlinear
features respectively, confirming that no single feature family dominates and that
careful preprocessing materially affects accuracy --- both observations that
shaped the preprocessing and feature pipeline of this project.

Across all reviewed works, a consistent pattern emerges: strong classification
accuracy is achievable, but the studies stop at the algorithmic result. None
deliver an accessible, end-to-end, multi-user web platform that a patient or a
primary-care physician could actually use. That translational gap is the central
motivation for this project.

\section{Methodology}
The methodology follows eight steps: (1) data acquisition using the UCI dataset
and a custom React web interface that captures audio via the browser
MediaRecorder API; (2) audio preprocessing (noise reduction, RMS normalization,
framing/windowing); (3) extraction of the 22-dimensional acoustic feature vector;
(4) KNN model development using scikit-learn with the Minkowski distance metric;
(5) systematic hyperparameter optimization over k, distance metric and weighting;
(6) integration of the serialized model into the Flask backend exposing REST
endpoints; (7) class-imbalance augmentation using a Conditional Tabular GAN
(CTGAN) to synthesize minority-class samples; (8) performance evaluation using a
held-out test set; and (9) continuous logging of predictions for monitoring and
future retraining.

\section{Overall System Description}
The Parkinson's Disease Detection system is a web-based machine-learning
application for automated screening through acoustic analysis of sustained vowel
phonation. It integrates a modern full-stack web application with a signal
processing backend and a KNN classification engine, accessible through standard
web browsers. The system comprises three principal subsystems operating in a
coordinated pipeline: the Frontend Web Application Subsystem (user interaction,
audio capture, result visualization), the Backend Processing Subsystem (data
transmission, preprocessing, feature extraction, API orchestration, persistence)
and the Machine Learning Inference Subsystem (KNN classification).

The system runs on standard computing hardware with internet connectivity,
without specialised medical equipment, and targets an end-to-end inference
latency under 500 ms. The data flow is sequential: the user records a sample in
the browser; the WAV file is transmitted to the Flask backend; the backend
preprocesses the audio and extracts the 22-feature vector; the KNN classifier
predicts the class and confidence; results are returned as JSON and rendered in
the interface, and persisted to the database for history tracking.

\section{Subsystem Specifications}
The platform is decomposed into cooperating subsystems, each with a clear
responsibility. This separation of concerns improves maintainability and allows the
components to be developed, tested and scaled independently.

\subsection{Client Device Subsystem}
The client subsystem is any browser-capable device used to access the application
and record a voice sample. Recording quality depends primarily on the microphone:
built-in laptop/phone microphones provide baseline quality (frequency response
roughly 100~Hz--10~kHz, signal-to-noise ratio 50--65~dB) sufficient for screening;
external USB condenser microphones offer improved quality (response to 20~kHz,
SNR above 70~dB) and are recommended; and professional audio interfaces with XLR
condenser microphones provide studio-grade capture for clinical settings. The
responsive frontend adapts to desktop, tablet and smartphone form factors.

\subsection{Server Infrastructure Subsystem}
The backend server hosts the Flask application, the trained model and the database.
A development configuration (quad-core CPU, 8~GB RAM) is adequate for a single
developer, while a production configuration (six-core CPU, 16~GB RAM, 1~Gbps
network) comfortably serves dozens of concurrent users. For higher load, the
stateless application can be replicated behind an Nginx reverse proxy / load
balancer, and the SQLite database can be migrated to PostgreSQL.

\subsection{Storage Subsystem}
The storage subsystem manages four classes of data: \emph{temporary audio}
(written to a temp file only for the duration of feature extraction, then deleted
to preserve privacy); the \emph{SQLite database} holding users, role profiles,
prediction history and appointments; the \emph{serialized model artifacts}
(\texttt{knn\_model.pkl}, \texttt{scaler.pkl}) loaded into memory at start-up; and
\emph{application logs} for monitoring and auditing.

\subsection{Machine-Learning Subsystem}
The ML subsystem encapsulates the trained KNN classifier, the feature scaler and
the feature-extraction pipeline. It exposes a single internal contract: given an
audio file path, return a label, class probabilities and the extracted feature
vector. This isolation means the model can be retrained or replaced (for example,
with the CTGAN-augmented model of Chapter~4) without changing the API layer.

\section{Working Principle}
The system operates through a continuous pipeline of audio acquisition, signal
processing, feature extraction, inference and presentation, organized into the
following phases:

\textbf{Phase 1 --- Authentication and session.} The user registers or logs in.
Registration captures demographic information (name, age, gender) stored in the
SQLite database. A JSON Web Token (JWT) is issued and used for subsequent
authenticated requests.

\textbf{Phase 2 --- Audio acquisition.} The browser MediaRecorder API captures a
sustained vowel /a/ for at least three seconds and encodes it as a WAV file,
transmitted to the backend \texttt{/api/patient/predict} endpoint.

\textbf{Phase 3 --- Preprocessing.} The backend loads the audio and applies
amplitude handling and resampling, preparing voiced segments for analysis (with
spectral-gating noise handling and RMS normalization as conceptual steps in the
pipeline).

\textbf{Phase 4 --- Feature extraction.} The 22-dimensional acoustic feature
vector is computed: fundamental-frequency measures, jitter (\%, absolute, RAP,
PPQ, DDP), shimmer (local, dB, APQ3, APQ5, APQ11, DDA), NHR, HNR, and nonlinear
measures (RPDE, DFA, spread1, spread2, D2, PPE). The vector is standardized using
the training-set scaler.

\textbf{Phase 5 --- KNN classification.} The standardized vector is classified by
the pre-trained KNN model using the Minkowski distance
$d(x,y) = \left(\sum_i |x_i - y_i|^p\right)^{1/p}$ (with $p=2$ for Euclidean
distance), majority voting among the $k$ nearest neighbours with distance
weighting, and a confidence score from the neighbour vote proportion.

\textbf{Phase 6 --- Result generation.} A JSON response with prediction, PD
probability, feature values and a clinical recommendation is returned.

\textbf{Phase 7 --- Visualization and persistence.} The frontend renders a
probability gauge, label and recommendation; the result is stored as a report.

\textbf{Phase 8 --- Logging.} Predictions are logged for monitoring and future
retraining.

The core inference logic is summarized in the following pseudocode.

\begin{verbatim}
FUNCTION Predict(audio_file, user):
    require user.role == PATIENT and valid JWT
    vector  = ExtractFeatures(audio_file)          # 22 biomarkers
    scaled  = scaler.transform(vector)
    pred    = knn.predict(scaled)                  # 0 = Healthy, 1 = PD
    proba   = knn.predict_proba(scaled)
    label   = "Parkinson's" IF pred == 1 ELSE "Healthy"
    SavePrediction(user.patient_id, label, proba)
    RETURN { label, pd_probability, healthy_probability, features }
END FUNCTION

FUNCTION KNN_Classify(x, k=5, metric="euclidean", weights="distance"):
    FOR each training instance t: d = Minkowski(x, t, p=2)
    neighbours = k smallest distances
    vote weighted by 1/d ; prediction = argmax(vote)
    RETURN prediction, confidence
END FUNCTION
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Class-Imbalance Augmentation with CTGAN}
The training data is imbalanced (training split: 103 Parkinson's vs 33 healthy,
a 3.12:1 ratio), which biases the classifier and depresses minority-class
(healthy) detection. To address this, a \textbf{Conditional Tabular GAN (CTGAN)}
was trained \emph{on the training split only} and used to synthesize 70 additional
healthy feature vectors, balancing the training set (33 $\rightarrow$ 103 healthy).
The held-out test set remained 100\% real and identical to the baseline split, so
the comparison is leakage-free. Synthetic-data quality was acceptable (no missing
values; mean per-feature Kolmogorov--Smirnov statistic 0.505, moderate given only
33 real healthy rows to learn from). The same KNN (k = 5, Euclidean, distance
weighting) was then retrained on the augmented data. Table~\ref{tab:ctgan} reports
the before/after comparison on the real test set.

\section{Performance Analysis}
The classifier exhibits strong sensitivity (recall), which is critical for
screening, where a false negative is more costly than a false positive. A
sensitivity of 93.55\% means roughly 94 of every 100 PD subjects are correctly
identified; the specificity of 88.46\% corresponds to correctly identifying about
88 of every 100 healthy subjects. The end-to-end latency of approximately 180 ms
is dominated by feature extraction (about 47\%) and preprocessing (about 25\%).
Scalability testing indicates acceptable response times up to roughly 50
concurrent users on a single instance, with horizontal scaling available for
higher load.

\textbf{Note on live-audio calibration.} The UCI features were produced with a
specific clinical pipeline (Kay Pentax MDVP and the authors' MATLAB code). The
open-source re-implementation used at inference (Praat/parselmouth and nolds) is
strongly correlated with, but not numerically identical to, that pipeline ---
especially for the nonlinear measures. Consequently, the reported metrics reflect
the model on UCI-distributed features, and predictions on freshly recorded audio
are approximate. The system is therefore a screening aid, not a diagnostic
instrument.

\section{System Advantages and Disadvantages}
\textbf{Advantages:} non-invasive screening; early-detection potential;
cost-effectiveness with an open-source stack; web accessibility across devices;
near real-time results ($\approx$180 ms); interpretability of KNN; longitudinal
report history; privacy (audio deleted after feature extraction); role-based
multi-user workflows; and containerized deployment.

\textbf{Disadvantages:} a small UCI dataset (195 recordings) with limited
demographic diversity; single-vowel analysis; class imbalance (3:1);
recording-quality dependence; absence of prospective clinical validation; linear
inference scaling of KNN with dataset size; a feature set limited to sustained
phonation; English-only design; uncalibrated confidence scores; and the
live-audio calibration gap described above.

% ===========================  CHAPTER 5  ==================================
\chapter{Conclusion and Future Scope}

\section{Conclusion}
This project successfully designed and implemented a web-based Parkinson's disease
detection platform using speech analysis and K-Nearest Neighbors classification.
A complete full-stack application was developed: a React (Vite + Tailwind)
frontend, a Flask REST API backend with JWT authentication and role-based access
control, an SQLite database, and a Python ML pipeline. A 22-dimensional acoustic
biomarker vector --- fundamental frequency, jitter, shimmer, NHR/HNR and the
nonlinear measures RPDE, DFA, spread1, spread2, D2 and PPE --- feeds a KNN
classifier optimized by grid-search cross-validation. The optimal configuration
(k = 5, Euclidean distance, distance weighting) achieves approximately 92\%
accuracy, 90\% precision, 93--100\% recall and an F1-score above 91\% on the UCI
dataset, with about 180 ms inference latency.

Beyond a predictor, the finalized system is a role-aware healthcare platform:
patients register, run voice screenings, view reports, find nearby hospitals via
OpenStreetMap and book appointments with verified neurologists; doctors review
reports, add diagnosis notes and manage appointments after administrator
approval; and administrators approve doctors and monitor platform statistics. The
project demonstrates that accurate, accessible PD screening is achievable on
standard hardware within a deployable web architecture, bridging the gap between
research prototypes and practical tools.

\section{Limitations}
Key limitations include the small and demographically narrow UCI dataset; the use
of sustained vowel /a/ only; class imbalance; sensitivity to recording quality;
the absence of prospective clinical validation; the linear inference scaling of
KNN; a limited acoustic feature set; English-only design; uncalibrated confidence
scores; the lack of comprehensive security auditing; and the absence of medical
device regulatory clearance. The live-audio calibration gap between the UCI
feature pipeline and the open-source extraction tools also constrains real-world
accuracy.

\section{Future Scope}
Several enhancements were implemented during finalization and move from
``future'' to ``done'': role-based access control, patient/doctor/admin
workflows, the OpenStreetMap hospital finder, appointment booking, generative
(CTGAN) data augmentation for class imbalance, and containerized (Docker)
deployment.

Promising future directions include: dataset expansion and diversification with
prospective, clinically labelled data; multi-task speech analysis (connected
speech, multiple vowels, diadochokinetic tasks); deep-learning integration and
ensembles; native mobile applications with on-device inference; real-time
recording-quality assessment; temporal-progression modelling for longitudinal
monitoring; multi-modal fusion with gait and handwriting biomarkers; prospective
clinical validation against UPDRS/DaTscan; a regulatory and quality-management
pathway (SaMD, ISO 13485, IEC 62304, ISO 14971); explainable-AI enhancements
(e.g.\ SHAP); telemedicine/EHR integration via HL7 FHIR with PDF report
generation and automated referrals; low-resource and multilingual global-health
deployment; and continuous/federated learning for privacy-preserving model
updates.
